**University of Kansas** · Undergraduate (GEOG 358) and graduate (GEOG 658) · Lecture + lab · Spring 2025 · Satisfies KU Core Goal 3 (Breadth of Knowledge)

## Course description

The broad goal of this course is to think critically about spatial phenomena, both natural and human, through spatial analysis, query, and visualization across geographic scales, from local and regional to global. The course covers the fundamental concepts of geographic information systems (GIS) and their applications in today's world: what GIS is, what it can be used for, and how to perform basic spatial analysis. Lectures explore the theories and ideas behind GIS, and weekly labs provide hands-on experience with **ESRI ArcGIS Pro** and an introduction to open-source **QGIS**.

## Topics

- What is GIS, and how geography is represented in it
- Map projections and coordinate systems
- Building a GIS database, raster and vector data, and attribute data
- Data collection (including field data collection) and data uncertainty
- Spatial queries, overlay, map algebra, and spatial analysis
- Geoethics
- Cartographic design, and Python in GIS

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the semester, students are able to:

1. Collect, store, analyze, and display spatial data;
2. Solve spatial problems using analytical techniques such as map overlay, queries, and statistical analysis;
3. Use ArcGIS Pro to create, edit, and query GIS vector data.

## Projects

Undergraduate students write a research proposal (which they can submit to KU's Undergraduate Research Awards) and complete an individual **final map project**, collecting, organizing, querying, and displaying spatial data in a printed map that they present to the class. Graduate students write a literature review on how GIS and spatial analysis have been used to study a research topic of their choice.
