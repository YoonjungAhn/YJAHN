**University of Kansas** · Undergraduate · 4 credit hours · Lecture + lab · Most recently offered Fall 2026

## Course description

This course introduces the benefits and limitations of using quantitative methods to analyze geographical problems. It covers traditional descriptive methods (e.g., measures of central tendency) and inferential statistics (e.g., hypothesis testing), as well as inherently geographical approaches such as shape and point pattern analysis and spatial autocorrelation. The laboratory emphasizes using computers to explore and analyze geographical problems, and the course introduces the powerful, open-source **R** scripting language for geographical data analysis.

## Topics

- Descriptive statistics: data structure, population and sample, central tendency and variability
- Probability
- Hypothesis testing: sampling, p-values, confidence intervals, and the Central Limit Theorem
- Temporal data visualization, scatterplots, and correlation
- Linear regression and interpreting results
- Spatial data analysis: spatial data types and structures, choropleth and density maps, point patterns, and spatial autocorrelation

## A research-intensive course

GEOG 316 is designed as a research-intensive course, supported by a KU Research-Intensive Course Mini-Grant. Students work with real data in R across 12 labs, explore an open-source R package and present it to their peers in an **Open Science** assignment, and develop their own **final research project**: a proposal (which students can submit to KU's Undergraduate Research Awards) and a presentation at KU's **Undergraduate Research Showcase**.
