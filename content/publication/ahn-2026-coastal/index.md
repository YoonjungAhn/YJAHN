abstract: We compare widely used global population grids (GPW, GPW-UN, GHS-POP, LandScan,
  and WorldPop) against U.S. Census block data from 1990 to 2020 to measure population
  in the low elevation coastal zone (LECZ, 0–10 m), and apply land-use refinements
  using NLCD and HISDAC-US. For 2020, national estimates vary by up to 6.4 million people
  (19% relative difference), with the unrefined overlay method systematically overestimating
  compared to spatially refined approaches. Coastal population growth consistently
  outpaced non-coastal growth across all datasets and periods, with LECZ growth rates
  2–7 percentage points higher than outside the LECZ.
